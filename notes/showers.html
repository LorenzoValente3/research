The showers on this site

The particles behind the main page are real Geant4 showers. Each one is a single particle in the OpenDataDetector, shot perpendicular to the beam from a radius of 1.2 m, just in front of the electromagnetic calorimeter (radius 1.25 to 1.5 m), towards the hadronic calorimeter (1.6 to 3.4 m). Starting there keeps the solenoid field from curling soft particles away inside the tracker. The simulation runs with DD4hep and ddsim from Key4hep, physics list FTFP_BERT.

What is simulated

Fourteen particle types, each with the same eight energies (1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100 and 200 GeV), one event per type and energy, 112 showers in total: e−, e+, γ, μ−, μ+, π+, π−, π0, K+, K−, K0L, p, p̄ and n. The page picks among them uniformly, so a muon that crosses everything and leaves a thin line is as likely as a 200 GeV pion that fills the hadronic calorimeter.

What is drawn

A side view, with the depth into the detector along the direction of flight. The lines are the Geant4 particles, drawn as straight segments from where they are created to where they stop: electrons and positrons in cyan, photons as white dashes, charged hadrons in amber, neutral hadrons as faint amber dots, muons in grey. A 200 GeV shower holds close to 300 000 particles, so each event keeps 220 of them, the most energetic half and an even sample of the rest. The dots are the 320 calorimeter cells with the most energy, cyan in the electromagnetic and amber in the hadronic calorimeter, larger for more energy.

Everything appears in the order of its Geant4 time, slowed down so that a particle at the speed of light crosses the screen in a few seconds. Slow neutrons keep depositing energy for microseconds after the prompt shower, and that late tail is compressed. A click fires one of the showers above 20 GeV at the click point, and the caption at the bottom of the main page names it.

Phones draw every second particle and cell. While the file loads, or if it does not, a simple Heitler-like cascade written in JavaScript takes its place. With reduced motion switched on in the system settings, nothing moves.

Code

All the code of this site, the showers included, is in its public repository, LorenzoValente3/research. Its README maps every file.

  • geant4/sim.sh: the Geant4 simulation with ddsim from Key4hep, one SLURM job per particle type.
  • geant4/convert.py: turns the EDM4hep output into showers.json, the 112 showers the page reads.
  • shower.js: the drawing, plain canvas, no libraries.
  • tests/: checks of shower.js without a browser, and screenshots of the site at several widths.

Why showers

Detailed simulation of showers like these takes most of the computing budget of a collider experiment, and my research is on generative models that learn to produce them much faster, for more than one calorimeter at once. The multi-geometry paper is the place to start.
