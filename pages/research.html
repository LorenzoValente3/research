Research

Broadly speaking my work is on Physics ∩ Machine Learning, and more precisely on generative models for the simulation of particle showers in calorimeters.

One model, many calorimeters. Detailed simulation with Geant4 dominates the computing budget of collider experiments, and learned surrogates have so far been tied to the one detector whose data trained them. I extend a flow matching transformer for shower point clouds with conditioning on the calorimeter geometry and pre-train it across several detectors. Fine-tuned on a detector it has never seen, it needs two orders of magnitude fewer Geant4 showers to match a model trained from scratch on a full dataset (arXiv:2608.18233).

Cross-geometry transfer. The first step was to show that a shower model trained on one calorimeter transfers to another with a fraction of the data (JINST 21 (2026) P07037).

Datasets and code. The pre-training data are produced with Geant4 and DD4hep for a family of sampling calorimeters (multi-calorimeter-dataset). Models and weights are public: AllShowers, PointCountFM, Hugging Face.

Current work: a numerical geometry descriptor as conditioning, towards a model that generalises to a detector without any fine-tuning.
